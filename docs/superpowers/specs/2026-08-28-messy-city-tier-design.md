# Messy-City Fixture Tier — Design Spec (DEL-24; cycle 3C part 1)

Date: 2026-08-28
Status: **approved by owner (2026-08-28)** — rev 2 after ultracode review
(16 findings → 13 confirmed → 6 root causes fixed; see decision log)
Branch: `del-24-messy-city` (off `origin/main` at b2e8e51)
Parent: WORKPLAN.md Phase 3 item "Add a second 'messy city' fixture tier"
[DEL-24]; the tier that proves any fix to DEL-19 (bbox adjacency) and DEL-20
(overlap double-count). Builds on the 3A spec § 4 (fixture families) and the
3B spec (categories, derived profiles). Conventions from the CI spec
(`generate_*_fixtures.py`, writes only under `tests/fixtures/`, `skipif` for
data) bind.

Decision log (brainstorm, 28 Aug 2026):
- Reference implementation is **generalised only**: parameterised over a
  city, all road rows summed, every rule untouched. Candidate fix rules
  (edge-only adjacency, single-assignment overlap) wait for DEL-19/20 after
  Raj, each with its own hand anchor.
- The tier pins **today's behaviour** on all six pathologies; the fix, when
  it comes, flips a pin deliberately.
- A data-gated **measurement script** gives the tier's real-data premises a
  reproducible source (`docs/data/layer_pathologies.md`).
- Structure: a `City` abstraction with two instances; Oraculum's files,
  numbers and hand anchors do not move.
- Rev 2 (ultracode review — reviewers built the city in shapely and ran
  both implementations on it): the no-population settlement is **split**
  from the RV settlement (`U` vs `N`) because production drops a
  no-population row unconditionally; scenarios carry **separate post- and
  pre-neighbour drop sets**; all **seven** services are placed so the
  invariants guard (spread AND unique clinic/school extremes) passes;
  bbox pins are stated **directionally**; `compute_city` takes an explicit
  scenario table; the measurement script never writes under `data_dir`.

## Purpose

Oraculum is small, rectangular and hand-ratifiable by design. The real
layer is none of those: 0 of 4,357 colonies are rectangles, 556 are
MultiPolygons, 4,050 pairs overlap, 360 have no neighbours, 15 have no
population row, areas span 2.3e-9 → 29 km². The messy city carries each of
those pathologies once, is scored by the independent reference
implementation (never hand arithmetic), and pins what production does
today on each — so that the adjacency and overlap fixes (DEL-19/20, after
Raj) are proven by a test that flips, not by argument.

## 1. Findings that shape the design (verified against the code)

- `border`/`touch` adjacency tests `geom_i ∩ geom_j` for `.length > 0`; for
  two **overlapping** polygons the intersection is a polygon whose
  `.length` is its perimeter, so overlapping pairs are `touch` neighbours.
  Recorded on DEL-19; pinned here. (Whether the eventual DEL-19 rule flips
  this pin depends on the definition Raj chooses; the pin records today.)
- Overlap double-counting is **agreed** behaviour: reference `within` and
  production `intersects` both count a point strictly inside two polygons
  for both. It is pinned by direct assertion, not by comparison.
- A MultiPolygon's centroid can lie outside its geometry (in the gap
  between parts); its bbox spans the gap.
- Production `minmax` raises `RuntimeWarning` on a constant column (fatal
  under `pytest -W error`); the reference returns 0.0. The messy city must
  give every service a non-degenerate PCEN spread (same invariant as
  Oraculum), enforced by the invariants guard.
- Production drops a no-population settlement from the reported frame
  **unconditionally** (every profile, every scenario — `dropped =
  excluded_ids ∪ missing`), keeping it in others' neighbour lists. The
  reference keeps such a row with a NaN denominator. So the messy city's
  **every** scenario must drop that id post-neighbours on the reference
  side — which is why the no-population pathology lives in its own
  settlement (`U`) and not in the RV one (`N`), and why a scenario carries
  post- and pre-drop sets separately (§ 3).
- `reference_impl._service_amounts` uses only the **first** road row; the
  emitter hard-codes the Oraculum loaders; scenarios drop literal ids.
  These three are the generalisation this cycle makes.
- `compute_frames` needs `area_km2` and a settlement whose category matches
  the profile's `exclusion.types` (`RV` for `code-2025`).

## 2. The city (`tests/fixtures/messy/`)

Generated by `scripts/generate_messy_fixtures.py`, deterministic
(`json.dumps(sort_keys=True, indent=1)`, no GDAL driver), EPSG:7760 metre
offsets from a base like Oraculum's, files `settlements.geojson`,
`services.geojson`, `barriers.geojson` (empty collection — no barrier
coverage in this tier), `expected_values.csv` (reference-emitted),
`production/<profile>.csv` (generator-emitted). Ten settlements:

| id | type | shape | pathology |
|---|---|---|---|
| `H` | Planned | irregular hexagon | non-rectangular; its envelope overlaps `L`'s geometry without touching → **bbox-only neighbour** |
| `L` | Planned | concave L-shape | non-rectangular; envelope-only relation with `H`; corner-only contact with `T` |
| `T` | Planned | triangle | **corner-only** contact with `L` (point intersection, length 0): neighbour under `bbox`, not under `touch` |
| `M` | Planned | MultiPolygon, two parts with a gap | centroid lies **outside** `M`, in the gap |
| `G` | Planned | small square inside `M`'s gap | disjoint from `M`'s parts, inside `M`'s envelope → `M ∈ nbrs_bbox(G)` but `G ∉ nbrs_bbox(M)` (an axis-aligned square is its own envelope; `M` never meets it) — the directed-bbox exhibit; near-zero centroid distance → contribution weight ≈ 1, the **undecayed** maximum |
| `O1`, `O2` | Planned | two rectangles overlapping in a strip | one clinic strictly inside the overlap → counted for **both**; `touch` neighbours via the overlap polygon's perimeter |
| `I` | Planned | square far from all | **isolated**: no neighbours under any rule |
| `N` | RV | ordinary square adjacent to `O2` | the settlement `code-2025` excludes (category exclusion, **with** a population) |
| `U` | Planned | ordinary square adjacent to `O1` | **no population row** (`population` NaN) — dropped by production unconditionally |
| `S` | Planned | 2 m × 1 m sliver adjacent to `H` | area 2e-6 km² (> 0 strictly); popdensity denominator ~5e7 → its PCEN is orders of magnitude below any other settlement that **owns** the same service |

Every settlement except `U` has a population (distinct values, so no two
settlements tie by construction); every settlement carries `area_km2`
computed analytically in the generator (as Oraculum does). **All seven
services are placed** — the reference scores every service in
`POINT_SERVICES` regardless, and production's PSI averages over the
services present, so both sides must carry the same seven: clinic in H, L,
T, M, G, O1∩O2 (the shared one), I; school in L, M, O2, N, S, **T and G**
(so no three-way tied zero under `touch`, where T, G and I have no
neighbours); bank in H, I; police in L, O1; ration in M, S; transport in
H, N. One road as **two** LineString rows crossing `H`, `L` and both parts
of `M` (exercises "sum all road rows"). The generator runs
`check_oraculum_invariants.check` on the emitted CSV — both checks: a
non-degenerate min-max spread for every (rule, scenario, denom, metric)
group, and a **unique** argmin/argmax for clinic and school — and refuses
to write a fixture that fails it. Vocabulary `messy-2` = `(Planned, RV)`.

## 3. `City` abstraction and reference generalisation

`tests/cities.py` (imports geopandas/pandas only — never `delhi_psi`, never
`reference_impl`):

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Scenario:
    name: str                        # reference scenario name
    dropped_post: frozenset[str]     # ids removed after neighbours are built
    dropped_pre: frozenset[str]      # ids removed from every neighbour list too
    exclusion_types: tuple[str, ...] # category names for the production side
    stage: str                       # post_neighbors | pre_neighbors (for the types)
    # Oraculum's five scenarios are expressed with one of the two sets empty,
    # exactly reproducing today's (dropped, dropped_before_neighbors) pairs.

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class City:
    name: str                       # "oraculum" | "messy"
    fixtures: Path                  # tests/fixtures/<name>
    scheme: str                     # "oracle-6" | "messy-2"
    vocabulary: tuple[str, ...]     # identity mapping = {t: t for t in vocabulary}
    scenarios: tuple[Scenario, ...]
    epsg: int = 7760
    def load_settlements(self) / load_barriers(self) / load_services(self)  # same shapes as today
    def mapping(self) -> dict[str, str]

ORACULUM = City(...)   # today's values verbatim: oracle-6, the 5 scenarios
MESSY = City(...)
CITIES = (ORACULUM, MESSY)
```

Messy scenarios — every one drops `U` post-neighbours, because production
does so unconditionally:

| name | `dropped_post` | `dropped_pre` | production side |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nopop_only` | `{U}` | ∅ | `types: []` — the missing-population drop alone |
| `excl_rv_post` | `{U, N}` | ∅ | `types: [RV]`, `post_neighbors` |
| `excl_rv_pre` | `{U}` | `{N}` | `types: [RV]`, `pre_neighbors` |

`nopop_only` and `excl_rv_post` differ on both sides (N reported or not),
so category exclusion is genuinely exercised on the messy city.
`tests/test_cities.py` pins, for every city and scenario, that production's
`excluded_ids(types) ∪ missing` equals `dropped_post ∪ dropped_pre` — the
id-vs-category agreement is asserted, not assumed.

`tests/oraculum_fixtures.py` keeps every public name as a thin wrapper
over city-taking functions: `oracle_config(base, city=ORACULUM)`,
`oracle_profile_path(base, directory, city=ORACULUM)`,
`compute_oracle_frame(profile, *, types, stage, denom, city=ORACULUM)`,
`methodology_with(...)`; `ORACLE_SCENARIOS`/`ORACLE_VOCABULARY`/`ORACLE_SCHEME`
become aliases of `ORACULUM.*`. No existing test changes its call shape or
expected value.

`tests/reference_impl.py`:
- `compute_city(..., scenario, ...)` currently resolves `SCENARIOS[scenario]`
  from a module global; it gains a keyword-only `scenarios: Mapping[str,
  Scenario] | None = None` (default: Oraculum's table) and applies
  `dropped_pre` to neighbour lists and `dropped_post` to the reported set /
  swallowed contributions — the same two mechanisms it has today, now
  independently settable. Oraculum numbers are unchanged (one set is
  always empty there).
- Backward-compatible module views, defined literally:
  `SCENARIOS = {s.name: (s.dropped_post | s.dropped_pre, bool(s.dropped_pre)) for s in ORACULUM.scenarios}`
  (the 2-tuple shape `test_reference_impl.py`, `render_oracle_maps.py` and
  `test_profiles_match_reference.py` read today) and, in
  `oraculum_fixtures.py`, `ORACLE_SCENARIOS = [(s.name, s.exclusion_types,
  s.stage) for s in ORACULUM.scenarios]` (the 3-tuple shape); both keep
  today's order. The two call sites that index `SCENARIOS` by name
  (`tests/test_profiles_match_reference.py` ~104–109,
  `scripts/render_oracle_maps.py` ~349) keep working through that view.
- `emit_expected_values(city, out_path)` reads `city.load_*()` and iterates
  `city.scenarios`; `_service_amounts` sums the intersection length over
  **all** road rows (Oraculum has one → no number changes); `__main__`
  writes both cities. Every rule, the min-max guard and the CSV format are
  untouched; Oraculum's `expected_values.csv` is byte-identical (row order
  and `%.17g` unchanged). The INDEPENDENCE RULE holds (`cities.py` is
  fixture plumbing, not index math).

`scripts/generate_production_fixtures.py`: `PROFILES × CITIES`, output
`tests/fixtures/<city>/production/<profile>.csv` — Oraculum's existing
path `tests/fixtures/oraculum/production/` is unchanged.

## 4. Proofs

1. **Reference match, both cities.** `test_profiles_match_reference.py`
   parametrised over `CITIES`: `compute_frames` under each shipped profile
   (mapped knobs) == the city's `expected_values.csv` block, every scenario
   × both reference denominators, `abs=1e-12`. `manuscript` on the messy
   city is proven only this way (no hand anchors — by design of the ticket).
2. **Production fixtures, both cities**, byte-stable, drift-guarded.
   Oraculum's `expected_values.csv` and both production CSVs are
   **byte-identical** to today (the reference change is a no-op there).
3. **Pathology pins** (`tests/test_messy_fixtures.py`, production side,
   direct assertions):
   - `bbox` (directed: `j ∈ nbrs(i)` iff `geom_i ∩ envelope_j ≠ ∅`):
     `L ∈ nbrs_bbox(H)` and `H ∈ nbrs_bbox(L)` (each envelope overlaps the
     other's geometry — the generator asserts this); `M ∈ nbrs_bbox(G)` and
     `G ∉ nbrs_bbox(M)`; `T ∈ nbrs_bbox(L)` and `L ∈ nbrs_bbox(T)`;
     `touch`: none of those pairs.
   - `O1 ↔ O2` neighbours under **both** rules (the DEL-19 finding).
   - `M.centroid` is not within `M`; and `G`'s clinic PCEN under `bbox`
     equals `(own + M's clinic count × 1/(1+d)) / denominator` with
     `d = dist(G.centroid, M.centroid)` in km — the near-zero-distance,
     weight-≈-1 case.
   - the overlap clinic: `clinic_count == 1` for **both** `O1` and `O2`
     (today's double count — the DEL-20 pin).
   - `I`: empty neighbour list under both rules; `clinic_pcen(I) == own /
     denominator` exactly.
   - `N`: reported under `nopop_only`, absent under `excl_rv_post` (so
     category exclusion is what removed it); present in `O2`'s neighbour
     list under `post_neighbors`, absent from it under `pre_neighbors`.
   - `U`: absent from the reported frame under **every** profile and
     scenario; present in `O1`'s neighbour list; `compute_frames(...,
     missing_population="error")` raises naming `U`.
   - `S`: `area_km2 == 2e-6`; its ration PCEN is the minimum among
     settlements that **own** a ration point and at least four orders of
     magnitude below `M`'s (settlements without the service and without a
     serving neighbour sit at exactly 0 — `I` always does).
   - geometry invariants: `M` has two parts; `area(O1 ∩ O2) > 0`; `I`
     disjoint from every other settlement; `S.area > 0`; `U.population`
     is NaN and `N.population` is not; `T ∩ L` is a point.
4. **Invariants guard** (`scripts/check_oraculum_invariants.py` →
   parametrised over cities, run by the generator before writing and by
   the pytest wrapper): (a) non-degenerate min-max spread for every (rule,
   scenario, denom, metric) group and (b) unique clinic/school
   argmin/argmax, in **both** CSVs.
5. **Divergence exhibit** unchanged.

## 5. Provenance: `scripts/measure_layer_pathologies.py`

`--data-dir` (read-only), applies the pipeline's dedup, then prints:
rectangles (polygon area == envelope area within 1e-9 relative),
MultiPolygons, isolated settlements under the `bbox` rule, settlements with
no population row (join against the population CSV on the pipeline's key),
area min/median/max in km², overlapping pairs (positive-area intersection)
and, per point-service layer, points inside more than one settlement.
The script **never writes under `data_dir`**: its dedup uses a
`--cache-dir` (default: a temporary directory), because `~/delhi_data` is
bisynced to the shared drive. Output committed as
`docs/data/layer_pathologies.md`: a prose header (run date, layer file,
commit) followed by one fenced ```text block of `key: value` lines with
fixed keys — `settlements`, `rectangles`, `multipolygons`, `isolated_bbox`,
`no_population`, `area_km2_min`, `area_km2_median`, `area_km2_max`,
`overlapping_pairs`, and `multi_settlement_points_<service>` per point
layer. `tests/test_layer_pathologies.py` is `skipif` without `~/delhi_data`,
runs the script with a `tmp_path` cache, parses that block, and compares
**counts only** (never date/file/commit). WORKPLAN's prose numbers become a
pointer to the doc.

## 7. Out of scope

Any rule change (edge-only adjacency, single-assignment overlap,
`partial_weighted`) — DEL-19/20/22 after Raj; multi-barrier `combine`
coverage (needs a second barrier layer in a city — its own small
follow-up); messy-city maps; a partial-canal case (memo § 2).

## 8. Autonomy and stopping rules

Same as 3A/3B: fix-forward/commit/push/merge yes once CI and § 4 proofs are
green; a confirmed Critical review finding governs over the plan. Stop and
report if any Oraculum fixture or the real-data baseline deviates, if a
carried-over test needs its expected value changed, or if the messy city's
reference and production disagree on any scenario (that would be a real
divergence, to be reported — not tuned away).
